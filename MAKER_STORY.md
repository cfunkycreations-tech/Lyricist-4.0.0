# Setting the Record Straight — The Christopher Funk Story

*The real story behind Lyricist and CFunky Creations LLC, in Christopher Funk's own words.*

---

Oh boy, Claude — you are something else, buddy. You take one thing I say and run with it. I'm talking about the shovel thing — how the richest men during the gold rush days were the ones selling the tools. That's true. And I appreciate you trying to use that metaphor, but it doesn't really capture what actually happened in my life.

So — discovering AI. I'd heard about it for years, heard a lot of talk on podcasts. I was watching Joe Rogan one day and he was talking about ChatGPT. I kept hearing about it all over YouTube, because I don't watch legacy media. Anyway — I checked it out, and I knew immediately. As soon as I started messing with it, I thought: holy shit. This is going to change the world. This is a paradigm shift in reality. I knew right then that this was what I was going to learn, and what I was going to do to make money.

Because two or three years before that, I became disabled. I couldn't work anymore. I kept trying to go back — they'd send me home, tell me I was a liability. I kept trying, kept getting sent back, until finally one day I said, "I guess I'm disabled then." And it's only gotten worse since. I've got degenerative disc disease, spinal stenosis, sarcoidosis, chronic sciatica in both legs, three ruptured discs, bone on bone. It sucks, man.

And that nearly killed me — not physically, but spiritually, mentally, emotionally. When a man loses his ability to work, he loses everything. Not just financially. Our culture is so messed up because of certain ideologies I'll just leave alone, but the people who think critically and think for themselves — they know what's going on. And that's why I don't think this country is going to fall the way a lot of people think it will, because people are waking up. Collective consciousness is powerful. There's 99% of us and 1% of them, and I'll be damned if I ever bow down.

So I taught myself how to code. I watched everything I could find on YouTube about AI and coding, and I still do to this day. I've got a daily digest hitting my inbox every morning — latest AI technology, scientific papers. I hang out on GitHub, Hugging Face, all the spaces where developers live. And once I started getting the hang of it, I said: I am an AI consultant. I am a full-stack developer. I am doing this. I might use AI to do it, but you better use AI to do it — because if you're not, you're going to get left behind.

AI is the biggest revolution we've ever had. Bigger than electricity.

My philosophy is simple. I had no audience at first. I started playing around with Suno, thought I could build something better with AI, started putting songs out there, got a small following. And I'm betting that once I put Lyricist out on these platforms and give it away free, people are going to be blown away — because it has real value. I know it does, because I use it myself. I wouldn't put it out there if it wasn't something I believed in. I'm not one of these kids using AI to clone somebody else's app and slap a price tag on it. This was my idea. Claude and I brainstormed it, added every feature one by one, built it into what it is — and it's still not done. I've got a lot of ideas left.

I'm giving it away free because I couldn't have afforded something like this a few years ago, and I want people to have it. Yes, I need to make money — I've got to do something. This is my career now. I work my ass off. Five hundred hours minimum into that app, and that's not counting the art, the decisions, everything else that goes into it.

I'm also a musician. Was a street musician — me and my brother, until our bodies started breaking down and he moved to Elgin. I still play at home. I've got a studio in my bedroom — Yamaha mixer, Ableton, microphone, five guitars, a MIDI controller, a Fender acoustic. I've been playing around with computers since I was ten years old. My first one was a TRS-80 from Radio Shack, back around '87 or '88.

I've been working since I was fifteen — construction, cooking, ironworker, drywall finisher, floor and tile. I've done pretty much everything in the trades. And I know that one day, the right developers are going to see my name and say: that's that Funk dude. Taught himself everything. Dropped out in ninth grade, got a GED, became disabled, and built something real. I'll take that respect when it comes.

I fail and fail and fail. So does every winner in life. They fail thousands of times before they win. I just keep going — because one day it's going to pay off. I don't do this just for the money. If I end up wealthy, I'll give most of it away. All I need is a small house. I'm a simple person. Material things don't matter to me. They don't come with you when you die.

The whole thing is like busking, honestly. Me and my brother used to play on the street — he played harmonica, I played guitar and sang — and we'd put a hat out, and people threw money in it. That's exactly what this is. I'm busking online with AI and my brain, because I had no other choice at first. But now I love it. I've turned something bad into something good.

And there's one more reason I do this, maybe the biggest one. I want to buy my mom a house. She's never had one. She's the toughest woman I've ever met in my life — West Texas born and raised, and that's where I get every ounce of my grit from. She had stage four lung cancer and beat it. She's eight years in remission. Not because of luck — because of her will, her strength, and her love for her kids. She lost my older sister in 1997 and it nearly destroyed her. She cried for two years. And then she woke up, took control of her life, got her driver's license, got a car, kept moving forward. That woman doesn't quit. Neither do I. That's not a coincidence.

People can overcome anything. It starts with how you think. What we think becomes real. You are what you think. You are who you hang around. You are who you say you are — and that's exactly why I am who I say I am. Not because a school said so. Not because of a certificate or a degree or anybody's approval. Because I said so. Because I taught myself. Because I decided.

I don't need your approval. I don't need anybody's approval.

I need mine.
